The Problem with Wood

Why Construction Needs
A Better Alternative

For more than 100 years, residential construction has relied primarily on wood framing systems built from lumber, plywood, and engineered wood products. While widely used, traditional wood construction presents significant environmental, safety, and durability challenges.

🌍 Environmental Impact
🌲
Deforestation

Global forests face continuous pressure from timber harvesting. Approximately 27 million acres of forest are lost worldwide each year, with wood-framed construction remaining one of the largest consumers of lumber and wood-based building materials.

🗑️
Construction Waste

Traditional construction generates millions of tons of job-site waste annually, much of which ends up in landfills — adding to environmental burden with every project completed.

💨
Carbon Emissions

Harvesting, processing, transporting, and replacing wood products consume substantial energy and natural resources, contributing to the carbon footprint of every wood-built structure.

⚠️ Safety Concerns
🔥
Fire Risk

Wood is combustible. Residential fires cause billions of dollars in property losses each year — and wood-frame structures ignite and spread flames rapidly, leaving little time to respond.

💧
Mold & Moisture Damage

Wood absorbs moisture, creating conditions for:

  • Mold growth
  • Rot and decay
  • Structural deterioration
  • Indoor air quality problems
🐛
Termites & Pests

Millions of homeowners spend billions of dollars annually on termite prevention and repair. Wood is inherently vulnerable to insect damage — a problem that steel eliminates entirely.

🏗️
Structural Limitations

Traditional wood-framed homes can experience:

  • Warping and shrinkage
  • Cracking and settling
  • Long-term deterioration

Real-World Precedent

Global Proof of
Metal Modular Construction

Steel-based modular construction isn't a concept — it's already been proven on the world stage. Micro-homes constructed from steel box units in Japan by architect Yasuhiro demonstrate the global viability of metal modular construction.

The "Cell Brick" project shows that precision-engineered steel units can be assembled into full living spaces — rapidly, repeatably, and beautifully. STEEL-ONE takes this proven concept and scales it for the American residential market.

What works for micro-housing in Japan works for full-scale homes in California — with the added advantages of fire resistance, earthquake performance, and speed that the US market demands right now.
